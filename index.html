About us

Students who build real rockets

We are the rocketry club at Edmonds College in Lynnwood, Washington.

What we do

Members design, simulate, build and launch their own rockets, from small kits to fiberglass airframes with electronics and a science payload.

The club exists to take STEM education outside the classroom, give students projects that strengthen their engineering portfolios and connect Edmonds students and faculty with area high school students. It also shows the community the quality of engineering education at Edmonds College.

Mission from the Edmonds College rocketry program page. Faculty advisor: William Hamp.

What is IREC

International Rocket Engineering Competition

IREC is an annual competition for student rocketry teams from the USA and around the world. It is run by the Experimental Sounding Rocket Association.

Teams build a rocket that carries a payload of at least 2.2 pounds and aim for a target altitude of 10,000, 30,000 or 45,000 feet. Rockets are typically 4 to 8 inches wide and 8 to 20 feet long. Solid, liquid and hybrid motors are all allowed. The event has run since 2006 and moved to Spaceport Midland in Texas in 2025.

Source: Experimental Sounding Rocket Association, What is IREC.

Our story

Small team, year-long project

Our 2025 launch team had five students, far fewer than most competitors. A storm damaged the site and only a few teams were allowed to fly, and we earned a launch slot. Judges praised our design, and Washington State University loaned spare parts when we needed them.

Source: Edmonds College Tritons blog and MLT News sponsor spotlight.

All figures come from the IREC 2025 final scoresheet.

Flight

Close to target and close to our model

We aimed for 10,000 ft. Dynamo reached 9,844 ft. Our simulation predicted 10,274 ft, so the real flight landed within 4.2 percent of the model.

Score breakdown

Strong flight, paperwork to fix

Each bar shows the points we earned out of the points available in that part of the score.

Flight performance481.5 / 500
Design and build177 / 240
Technical report, before penalty123.3 / 200
Entry and progress updates30 / 60
Late or administrative penalty-200 pts
Where we lost the most. A 200-point late or administrative penalty on the technical report, and zero points on two of the three progress updates. Without that penalty our score would have been about 811.8, which would have put us in the top 30 of the 87 teams in 10K COTS.

None of this was about the rocket. Paperwork is the most fixable part of our result, and our 2027 timeline schedules every deadline early.

Use of funds

Where your gift goes

  • Airframe and materials

    Fiberglass or carbon tubes, nose cone, fins, epoxy and hardware.

  • Motor and propellant

    Certified motors sized for our target altitude, plus test and spare units.

  • Avionics and payload

    Flight computers, GPS trackers, sensors and the science payload.

  • Recovery system

    Parachutes, deployment charges and shock cord.

  • Test launches

    Field fees, travel and supplies for the flights that prove the design.

  • Travel and registration

    Competition entry, shipping the rocket and lodging for the team.
